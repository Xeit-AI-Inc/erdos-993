# Evidence Policy

## Proof Claims

A claim labeled formal must identify the exact Lean declaration, pinned
toolchain, source, kernel check, axiom check, and intended mathematical scope.
Formalization of a weaker or differently guarded statement does not establish
the intended theorem.

## Computational Claims

A computational result must include:

- exact input scope;
- deterministic source code;
- exact arithmetic where the claim requires it;
- software and dependency versions;
- replay instructions;
- a machine-readable result or certificate;
- an explicit statement of what was not searched.

A finite null search is evidence, not a universal theorem.

## Counterexamples

A proposed counterexample must provide canonical graph data, its exact
independent-set sequence, the relevant valley indices, and an independent
recomputation. A counterexample to an auxiliary mechanism is not automatically
a counterexample to Erdős #993.

## Model-Generated Material

Language-model output is treated as candidate mathematics. It receives no
credit until its claims pass the same proof, computation, citation, and
fidelity requirements as human-generated work.

## Corrections

Errors remain visible in history. Corrections must identify the original
scope, the defect, the revised claim, and the verification that supports it.
