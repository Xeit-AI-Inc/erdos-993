## What is structurally useful

The exact margin (q-j-1)T_i[j]C[j+1]>=(q-j)T_i[j+1]C[j] is formally verified for arbitrary finite products of the three permitted branch factors. The proof uses H=qC-zC' and its nonnegative degree-down decomposition, 15 local likelihood-ratio comparisons, convolution and summation. It has no census, assumed local comparison, or assumed LC hypothesis on its face. This is the strongest structural contribution of the run.

At eligible lower-half ranks C[j+1]<C[j]. The margin controls relative decay but the aggregate contains a positive binomial slope. In exact notation A=sum r_i e_i T_i[j], b=e0+sum r_i e_i and delta=q-j, one obtains S<=b Delta_j L^N-A/delta. Thus A>=b delta Delta_j L^N is a sufficient absolute-mass theorem. The relative margin does not imply this lower bound. Rewriting that same estimate as a covariance inequality does not add the missing absolute information.

The weaker exact-ratio payment A[delta-(delta-1)C[j+1]/C[j]]>=b delta Delta_j L^N also suffices. Actual descent supplies an intermediate discount. These are alternative sufficient targets, not claims that MASS is necessary for a proof. A successful continuation should decide which target has a provable uniform mechanism, rather than simply accumulate equivalent formulas.

## What the failures teach

The accepted aggregate already rules out endpoint-only selection in the lower half, because it would give S=Delta_j L^N>0. This consequence inherits the computer-assisted dependency. An independent selector lemma is needed only if that route is used to replace the census. Calling the mathematical possibility simply OPEN was imprecise; the remaining gap concerns an independent proof.

A stronger selector shortcut, demanding a nonnegative shifted cofactor slope, is unnecessary and fails at (a2,a3,a4)=(0,12,10),p = 39. Every branch is selected while both represented cofactor slopes are negative. The correct threshold is relative to the negative endpoint-deletion slope. This emphasizes retaining the exact strict inequality instead of strengthening it into a convenient sign condition.

The empty-plus-singleton center-choice expansion is an exact lower bound, but its proposed universal payment fails at arity 4, m = 150,x = 292, p = 294 with all 601 tags selected. Its mass/debt ratio is about 0.921701 while the full MASS ratio is about 854.729. This is lost mass from truncation, not a near-counterexample to MASS. Adaptively retaining more center-choice layers is plausible; a fixed two-layer payment theorem should be retired.

The eligible 24-branch arity 4 occupation witness has positive pointwise full-mark birth drift but negative aggregate and negative conditional mean drift. Therefore a pointwise drift proof cannot work at that scope. Averaged estimates remain possible. Moreover E_jD<-1 everywhere in the actual eligible lower-half domain; the easier E_jD>=-1 sufficient case never occurs there. Use the actual target regime when assessing covariance arguments.

Spread remains a plausible second structural route, but it has three obligations: preserve eligibility via first-descent nondecrease, compare complete selected S at common ranks, and prove the balanced ancestor independently without the census. The exact cofactor stencil is a reformulation. A reversed binomial sign in its displayed version must be corrected; direct parent replay alone did not test that display. None of these facts justifies treating finite successful spreads as a proof.
