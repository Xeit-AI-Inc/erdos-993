## The strongest surviving result and its dependencies

For the exact uniform family `T_m` consisting of path `a-b-r` and `m` centers adjacent to `r`, each with three private tips, the T and F adjudicators supply an all-`m` proof with a **computer-assisted finite base**, not a kernel certificate. For every `m>=1` and every integer `p` with `x(T_m)+2<=p` and `3p<2alpha(T_m)+1`, both original leaf orbits are strictly favorable and the complete sum over the path leaf and all `3m` distinct tips is strictly negative. At `m=1,2` there is no eligible rank. This result is confined to this family.

The graph decomposition in both adjudications agrees: with `L=1+z`, `B=L^3+z=1+4z+3z^2+z^3`, `P=I(T_m)=(1+2z)B^m+zL^(3m+1)`, `A0=I(T_m-a)=LB^m+zL^(3m)`, and `A*=I(T_m-c)=(1+2z)(1+3z+z^2)B^(m-1)+zL^(3m)` for a private tip `c`. The marked differences are `q_a=zL^(3m)` and `q_c=z(2+5z+2z^2)B^(m-1)+zL^(3m)`. Thus `Q=q_a+3m q_c=zR`, where `R=3m(2+5z+2z^2)B^(m-1)+(3m+1)L^(3m)`. Once the two strict selectors are proved, `S(T_m,p)=R_(p-1)-R_(p-2)` is the **complete** sum. The F adjudicator's T22 arithmetic independently uses these same formulas; the U adjudicator corrected a distinct tip-deletion error in its bounded replay.

The finite part is reproducible exact integer arithmetic at `3<=m<=999`, for each central `j=m..2m+2`: the adjudicators report 1,507,464 positive log-concavity margins for `Z=A0,A*,R`, 1,507,464 positive mixed minors `Z_jP_(j+1)-Z_(j+1)P_j`, and 276,938 eligible row checks of both selectors and complete `S`, with no failure. The `B^m` recurrence and boundaries appear in both reports; the pinned `C5-AT/RESULTS.json` records minima 1210 and 1243. This is a finite computer check, not a universal induction or Lean proof. I checked the sealed evidence counts and formulas but did not rerun all 3,014,928 margins.

For `m>=1000`, the adjudicators give a self-contained analytic outline with explicit constants. The main factors `L`, `1+2z`, `B`, `1+3z+z^2`, and `2+5z+2z^2` are ULC of orders `1,1,4,2,2` respectively; notably `B` is used as ULC(4), not ULC(3). Direct normalized-coefficient and derivative checks pass. [Gurvits, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0804.1181) indeed states convolution of ULC(`l`) and ULC(`d`) nonnegative sequences is ULC(`l+d`); the stated factors have the required nonnegative coefficients and interval support. This gives the required central main-part LC margins. The `h=8` term of `B^(m-1)` bounds the binomial perturbation by `epsilon=8!(125/124)^8(150/49)^27/m^8`. The four exact comparisons `24(2003)K/1000^8<1`, `47(2003)K/1000^8<1`, `431(2003)K/1000^8<1`, and `(14688/21)(2003)K/1000^8<1`, with `K=8!(125/124)^8(150/49)^27`, pass independent rational arithmetic. All coefficient minors of the three short factor pairs are nonnegative, with leading minors `1,1,21m`; LC of the shared factor gives the Cauchy–Binet main mixed-minor bound. The reports identify and bound the adverse cross terms by `47epsilon`, `431epsilon`, and `14688m epsilon` times the appropriate squared main coefficient. The cutoffs and decreasing `(2m+3)/m^8` close all central LC and mixed-minor inequalities for every `m>=1000`.

The second import is the common-pinned `Erdos993G1.forest_descent_linear_bound`: the source declaration has no `DlHyp` binder, and its theorem contract and verification receipt give the finite-forest implication `Delta_k<0 => n<=4k`. I checked its pinned statement and receipt, without running Lean. At `k=x(T_m)`, `4m+3<=4x`, hence `x>=m+1`; eligibility gives `p<=2m+1` and `x<=2m-1`. The first descent therefore lies in the central interval, as do the needed propagation ranks. At `j=x`, positive mixed minors and `P_(x+1)<P_x` force descent of both deletion polynomials and `R`. Their strict LC propagates those descents to the selector rank `p` and to `R_(p-1)<R_(p-2)`. This bridge uses no log-concavity or no-recovery assumption for `P`.

I recommend carrying the all-`m` family assertion as **proposed computer-assisted verification with the two cited imports**, conditional only on the already pinned forest verification being accepted at its exact scope. It is stronger than the U adjudicator's corrected `m<=40` check but has a different evidence grade from a fully formal family theorem. It has no implication for arbitrary ordinary trees or heterogeneous branch profiles.

## Unresolved universal obligation

For the **fixed strict** favorable set `F`, original supports and distinct leaf tags, put `k=p-1`, `Q=sum_F q_v(k)`, `U=sum_F q_v(k+1)`, `E` the sum of actual addable degrees, `C` the count of multiply marked upper sets once per tag, and `D=sum_A(2(a-1-k)-e(A))`. The marked incidence argument gives `E=kU+C` and the exact identity

`kS=E-C-kQ=(2a+1-3p)Q-D-C`.

The lower-region coefficient is strictly positive. Proving the registered assertion requires `D+C>=(2a+1-3p)Q` for **every eligible ordinary tree and its complete fixed selected family**, or an equivalent bound; alternatively, a refutation requires an eligible tree with positive complete `S` and full exact selector data. The all-`m` theorem, bounded heterogeneous boxes, valid star sector, local positive T22 tag, matching and shell identities, and low-cover witness do not supply either universal resolution. No vote or source self-award changes that status.
