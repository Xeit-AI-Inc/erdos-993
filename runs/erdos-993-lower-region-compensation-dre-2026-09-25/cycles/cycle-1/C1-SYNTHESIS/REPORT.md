## Exact question and common notation

For a finite ordinary tree `T`, let `i_j(T)` count independent `j`-sets with integer zero extension, `Delta_j(T)=i_(j+1)(T)-i_j(T)`, `a=alpha(T)`, and `x(T)=min{j>=0:Delta_j(T)<0}`, checked through `j=a`. For a natural `p` with `x(T)+2<=p` and **strict** `3p<2a+1`, fix `F(T,p)={v: v is an original leaf and Delta_p(T-v)<0}`. Using each original support `s_v` and original closed neighborhood `N_T[s_v]`, the registered primary target is

`S(T,p)=sum_(v in F(T,p)) [Delta_(p-1)(T-{v,s_v})-Delta_(p-1)(T-N_T[s_v])]<=0`.

Each original leaf is a separate tag, including leaves at a common support. The empty sum is zero. Eligibility gives `p>=2` and `p<a`. The high-tail region, all-rank R23 aggregate, governed target, and support-fibre target remain separate claims.

For each original leaf put `H_v=T-{v,s_v}`, `W_v=N_T(s_v)\{v}`, `R_v=T-N_T[s_v]=H_v-W_v`, `k=p-1`, and `q_v(j)=i_j(H_v)-i_j(R_v)`. The last number counts independent `j`-sets of `H_v` meeting `W_v`. The exact selected sum is

`S=sum_(v in F)[q_v(k+1)-q_v(k)]`.

The separately registered bipartite tagged-incidence identity has a direct proof at its exact scope. For finite bipartite `H`, arbitrary marked `W`, integer `h>=alpha(H)`, and `1<=k<=h`, let `q_j` count independent `j`-sets meeting `W`, `e(A)` count the **actual** addable vertices of a marked independent `k`-set, `D=sum_A[2(h-k)-e(A)]`, and `C` count each independent `(k+1)`-set with at least two marks once. Then

`k q_(k+1)+C=2(h-k)q_k-D`, with `D,C>=0`.

The proof in EVIDENCE.md checks the endpoints, bipartite capacity and marked-deletion multiplicity. In the tree specialization `alpha(H_v)=a-1`, so it yields the exact equality

`kS=(2a+1-3p)sum_(v in F)q_v(k)-sum_(v in F)(D_v+C_v)`.

Because `2a+1-3p>0`, its nonnegative deficits alone do not prove the target. The missing global budget is exactly

`sum_(v in F)(D_v+C_v)>=(2a+1-3p)sum_(v in F)q_v(k)`.

This budget is equivalent to `S<=0`; it remains unproved by the adjudicated work. A proof by another method or an eligible ordinary tree with positive **complete** `S` would also resolve the primary.

## Reconciliation and recommendation

The T and U proofs of the tagged-incidence identity agree, and the F adjudication gives the same elementary count as context. Its edge cases `k=h`, `q_k=0`, and non-tight `h` are covered. I propose that the controller review the separately registered auxiliary as an exact proof at its stated finite-bipartite scope. This recommendation does not transfer a verdict to the primary.

The T and U star results agree: `I(K_(1,m),z)=(1+z)^m+z`, `a=m`, and `x=ceil(m/2)` for `m>=4`; every eligible star has all `m` leaves favorable and `S=m[binom(m-1,p)-binom(m-1,p-1)]<0`. The eligible `K_(1,12),p=8` control gives all 12 selector values `-110`, all terms `-165`, and `S=-1980`. This is a proper subfamily theorem. The support-fibre and decorated-core recurrences are compatible descriptions of the same original deletion operations.

The F results are finite observations or an abstract relaxation, not a conflicting universal theorem. The two pinned larger controls have one positive selected term each but negative **complete** sums. I re-summed their 67 and 181 stored terms and checked the recorded guards; I did not regenerate their graph coefficient vectors. Their positive tag rules out any universally feasible inclusion matching confined to each tag; it does not rule out aggregate compensation. The distinct order-22 R28 slot-dominance refutation is not an ordinary full-sum witness for this target.

The corrected F5 coefficient calculation is `Delta_p(T-v)=q_(p+1)-q_p+r_(p+1)-r_(p-1)`, whereas its summand is `q_p-q_(p-1)`. This reconciles F5 with U4 and prevents use of the erroneous `r_p` variant. The F2 result is tied to the executable generator rule `2d+2`; changing that rule would define a different sample.

**Proposed controller action:** review the direct proof of the registered bipartite tagged-incidence identity at its exact scope; retain the primary lower-region target as unresolved on this cycle's evidence; keep the finite searches, local-positive controls, and star theorem at their stated scopes. The next mathematical step is a proof of the fixed-selector global deficit budget (or an equivalent compensation invariant across supports/tags), or a reproducible eligible tree with all selector values, coefficient vectors and positive complete `S`. A search can use the fixed-`a` bound and support-fibre recurrence, but finite negative results cannot establish the unbounded theorem. Any full witness must recheck terminal-rank `x`, both strict guards, original deletion operations and all selected leaf tags.
