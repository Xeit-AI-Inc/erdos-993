## Target and common algebra

For an ordinary finite tree `T`, let `a=alpha(T)` and let `x` be the first strict descent of the integer zero-extended independence sequence, including the terminal rank. For `x+2<=p` and strict `3p<2a+1`, fix **once** the original-leaf selector `F(T,p)={v: Delta_p(T-v)<0}`. Every leaf, including leaves with a common original support, is a distinct tag. Put `H_v=T-{v,s_v}`, `W_v=N_T(s_v)\{v}`, `R_v=T-N_T[s_v]=H_v-W_v`, and `q_v(j)=i_j(H_v)-i_j(R_v)`. With `k=p-1`, `Q=sum_F q_v(k)` and `U=sum_F q_v(k+1)`, the exact complete sum is `S=U-Q`.

Actual addable vertices of marked independent `k`-sets give `E=kU+C`, where `C` counts each multiply marked upper set once **per selected leaf tag**. With `h=a-1`, `D=sum_(v,A)[2(h-k)-e_v(A)]`, the already reconciled marked-incidence identity yields

`kS=(2a+1-3p)Q-D-C`.

The coefficient is positive in this strict lower region. Thus the arbitrary-tree primary is still OPEN: its equivalent missing implication is `D+C>=(2a+1-3p)Q` for the fixed selector. Neither nonnegativity of `D,C`, a positive or negative local term, nor a bounded negative full sum resolves it. T4 and U4 sharpen the exact algebra, but neither proves this global budget or supplies an eligible tree with positive **complete** `S`.
