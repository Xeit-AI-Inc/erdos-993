# C5 preparatory direction audit — independent, unawarded

**Disposition.** The proposed `T_m` log-concavity/likelihood-ratio (LC/LR) package is a valid *conditional* sufficient route to the exact ordinary-tree selected sum, but it proves none of its six universal hypotheses. It is neither the r19 four-ratio theorem nor an all-`m` advance. The maximum-extension formula is correct, but by itself is an exact rewrite of addability; the useful new face below is a matching-slack decomposition that exposes where extension deficit can arise. No claim status or authority is changed.

## The `T_m` package and its true rank use

Write `P=I(T_m)`, `K=Q_all=zR`, and `D_0=A0`, `D_*=Astar` with the polynomials exactly as in `C4-TM-LR-SUFFICIENT-PACKAGE.md`. Root conditioning in r19 F1 confirms all three displayed formulas. Degrees are `deg P=3m+2=a`, `deg D_0=deg D_*=3m+1`, and `deg R=3m`, and every coefficient from rank zero through each degree is positive. The lower-region guard gives the integer bound `p<=2m+1`. If an eligible `p` exists, `x<=p-2<=2m-1`; hence `x,x+1,p,p+1` lie within the positive deletion support, and `x,x+1,p-2,p-1` lie within the positive `R` support. This handles terminal zeros without an unproved boundary convention. Direct coefficient calculation gives no eligible rank at `m=1,2` (`x=2,4`, respectively); the inequality argument works whenever eligibility holds, including `m>=3`.

For a positive coefficient sequence `Z`, let `rho_Z(j)=Z_(j+1)/Z_j`. The mixed minor `Z_j P_(j+1)-Z_(j+1)P_j>=0` is exactly `rho_Z(j)<=rho_P(j)`. At `j=x`, `rho_P(x)<1` by the definition of first *strict* descent, so `rho_Z(x)<1`. LC of `Z` makes its adjacent ratios nonincreasing up to the last positive coefficient; the next zero is also a descent. Thus `Delta_j Z<0` for every `j>=x` through its support. Applied to `D_0,D_*` at `j=p`, both original leaf orbits are strictly favorable. Applied to `R` at `j=p-2`, it gives

`S(T_m,p)=Delta_(p-1)K=R_(p-1)-R_(p-2)<0`.

No LC of `P` and no no-recovery assertion for `P` is used. Indeed, only **three minors at the crossing rank `x`**, one for each `Z`, plus the three LC tails `j>=x` are needed for this proof. The proposal's all-rank mixed minors and full-support LC are stronger quantifier ranges than this minimum. Conversely, replacing all-rank minors with crossing minors does not supply r19's post-crossing ratio package; it is a different conditional proof.

### Exact r19 comparison

The actual r19 F1 package uses `I=P`, `K=zR`, `M_0=D_0`, `M_*=D_*`:

| r19 condition | Exact indices in F1 | New condition and relationship |
|---|---|---|
| R-I | `P_(r+1)<P_r`, `x<=r<a=3m+2` | Absent from the new package. The first descent at `x` is used, with LC of each auxiliary sequence replacing later `P` descent. |
| R-0, R-* | `D_(r+1)P_r<=D_r P_(r+1)`, `x<=r<a` | Same orientation as the proposed deletion mixed minors, but the proposal asks all support ranks and additionally asks LC of each deletion polynomial. Its proof actually uses only `r=x` plus LC tails. |
| R-K | `K_r P_(r-1)<=K_(r-1)P_r`, `x+1<=r<=a` | The proposed `R/P` minor at `j` is `K_(j+1)P_(j+1)-K_(j+2)P_j>=0`. This is **not** R-K under a rename: `K` and `P` have shifted relative indices. LC of `R` is an extra obligation. |

R19's proof uses R-I at `r=p,p-1`, deletion minors at `r=p`, and R-K at `r=p` for an eligible nonterminal `p`. In contrast the new proof propagates three crossing-rank inequalities from `x` by auxiliary LC. R-I plus R-K and LC of `P` would imply the new `R/P` minor on the relevant tail (R-K at `r=j+2`, then monotonic `P` ratios), but neither LC of `P` nor LC of `R` is part of r19's four hypotheses. There is no logical implication between the packages as stated.

For a transparent abstract rank check, take `P=(1,2,3,2.7,0.54)`, so `x=2`, and `R=(1,1.5,1.5,1.2,0.12)`. `R` is LC and every defined `R/P` mixed minor is nonnegative (ratio lists `2,1.5,0.9,0.2` for `P` and `1.5,1,0.8,0.1` for `R`). Yet with `K=zR`, r19 R-K fails at `r=4`: `K_4/K_3=0.8>0.2=P_4/P_3`. This is a logical sequence example, **not** a `T_m` polynomial or counterexample. Conversely, with the same `P`, choose `K_2=1,K_3=0.01,K_4=0.001` and any positive `K_1`; R-K holds at `r=3,4` (`0.01<=0.9`, `0.1<=0.2`), while `R=z^{-1}K` has successive ratios `0.01,0.1` and fails LC. This illustrates the separate LC debt.

The r19 F adjudication retains the deletion-ratio package only for its independent `m<=16` check; its `513..2048` replay omitted both deletion-ratio tests. The recent `m<=400` root diagnostic is smaller than that aggregate bounded frontier, has different checks, and establishes no universal LC or minor. Neither source licenses a universal `T_m` selector theorem.

## Maximum-extension defect: identity, obstruction, and a new local lemma

For a selected leaf tag `v`, let `H_v=T-{v,s_v}`, `W_v=N_T(s_v)\{v}`, `k=p-1`, and let `A` range over independent `k`-sets of `H_v` meeting `W_v`. Put `B=A∪{v}`; then `B` is an independent `p`-set of `T`, and its addable vertices are exactly the addable vertices of `A` in `H_v`. Let `w_F(B)` count these *selected* tags producing `B`, and `Q=Σ_B w_F(B)`. Tagged extension counting from the optional contract gives `E=Σ_B w_F(B)e_T(B)=kΣ_v q_v(k+1)+Σ_v C_v`. Thus `kS=E-C-kQ`, where `C=Σ_v C_v>=0`. With `J_B=T-N_T[B]`, `nu_B=ν(J_B)` and `L_B=a-p-α(J_B)>=0`, the forest identity `α(J_B)=|J_B|-nu_B` gives `e_T(B)=a-p+nu_B-L_B`. Consequently

`kS=(a-2p+1)Q-Σ_B w_F(B)(L_B-nu_B)-C`.

This is exact and does not make `L_B-nu_B` nonnegative. It is algebraically equivalent to the addability budget, so a proof must supply a **new selected weighted lower bound**, not simply rename `E` as `Dext`. Likewise, with `n=|T|`, `U_B=N_T(B)` (open neighborhood), `e_T(B)=n-p-|U_B|` and hence `kS=(n-2p+1)Q-Σ_Bw_F(B)|U_B|-C`. In the `H_v` variables, `e_H(A)=n-p-1-|N_{H_v}(A)|`; because `N_T(B)=N_{H_v}(A) ⊔ {s_v}`, the equivalent formula is `kS=(n-2p)Q-Σ_(v,A)|N_{H_v}(A)|-C`. Thus the root neighborhood note's `n-2p` coefficient is correct when its neighborhood sum is understood in `H_v`, and the domain should be written explicitly.

**Exact matching-slack lemma (new local preparation lemma, not an award).** Let `T` be any finite forest, `B` any independent set, `U=N_T(B)`, `J=T-N_T[B]`, and `M` any maximum matching of `T`. Write `u_M(U)` for the number of vertices of `U` uncovered by `M`, `b_M(U)` for the number of edges of `M` with both endpoints in `U`, and `M_J=M∩E(J)`. Then

`a(T)-|B|-α(J) = u_M(U)+b_M(U)+(ν(J)-|M_J|).`  (★)

All three terms on the right are nonnegative. **Proof.** For a finite forest `F`, `α(F)=|V(F)|-ν(F)`: induct on vertices. An isolated vertex contributes one to `α` and zero to `ν`. Otherwise choose a leaf `l` with neighbor `t`. Some maximum independent set contains `l` (replace `t` by `l` if necessary), and some maximum matching contains `lt` (if `l` is unmatched, add or swap `lt`). Hence both maxima recur on `F-{l,t}`, with increments one. Since `|J|=|T|-|B|-|U|`, substitution gives the left side `|U|-ν(T)+ν(J)`. Let `t_M=|M\E(J)|`. Every edge outside `J` meets `U` because `B` is independent and no edge joins `B` to `J`. Matching edges of this type use either one or two distinct `U` vertices, so `|U|-t_M=u_M(U)+b_M(U)`. Also `ν(T)=|M_J|+t_M`. Substitute these two equalities to obtain (★). ∎

The lemma identifies three specific ways extension deficit appears; it does **not** subtract the residual matching `ν(J)` required by the aggregate. A useful certificate must charge the selected average of these three slacks against `ν(J)` and the positive budget, possibly using `C` as a second credit. At the r19 `T_22` witness, take `p=34` and `B={root, marked-arm leaf} ∪` any 32 of the 66 claw leaves. The root meets the arm's mark `W={root}`, so this is a selected arm tag (r19 records all 67 original leaves favorable). `J_B` consists of the other 34 isolated claw leaves; `a=68`, `ν(J_B)=L_B=0`, and `e_T(B)=34>k=33`. Thus a per-set bound `e_T(B)<=k`, or a universal positive `L_B-ν_B`, is false at a genuinely selected tag. Its arm summand is positive while the complete r19 aggregate is negative. Any matching certificate must transfer credit across tags or across sets.

## Other proposals: substantive next tests

* **Selector identities.** The two deletion formulas in `C4-ROOT-SELECTOR-QUESTION.md` check algebraically. A nonselected leaf is characterized exactly by `Delta_(p-1)H_v<=Delta_p T`. This is useful for a *proper-selector witness*, but it does not imply any sign for `g_v`, and a theorem that all leaves are favorable would be stronger than the target. The next result needs an exact tree/rank witness or a proved structural exclusion, not a restatement of the identities.
* **Neighborhood components.** For independent `A⊂H_v`, the incidence graph on `A∪N_H(A)` containing only edges incident to `A` is a forest. If it has `c_A` components (including isolated `A`), then `|N_H(A)|=Σ_(u∈A)deg_H(u)-|A|+c_A` and `e_H(A)=|H|-Σdeg_H(A)-c_A`. Both are exact. An average *upper* degree bound points the wrong way for the required lower bound on the weighted degree-plus-component sum. The next result must prove such a lower bound under early descent and the fixed selector, with a case showing it beats the verified first-order shell.
* **Post-signed-map direction.** Boundary-of-boundary cancellation defeats a signed simplicial deletion map, including any retagging determined only by the final face. A characteristic-zero unsigned map is a different candidate, but the noted T22 local unsigned-injectivity failure requires explicit cross-tag transfer. A substantive result is a fully specified operator with domain/codomain bases indexed by the fixed selected tags, an exact kernel or rank inequality implying `S<=0`, and a tested nontrivial row. Merely invoking Lefschetz theory, generic weights, or a Hall reformulation supplies no theorem.
* **Quantitative coefficients and saddle points.** The displayed recurrence for `b_(m,j)=[z^j]B^m` is correct by coefficient comparison of `z(B^m)'=mzB'B^(m-1)`, but its `4(m-j)` coefficient changes sign around central ranks. There are two distinct meanings of ULC here: `B=(1,4,3,1)` fails binomial-normalized ULC of order 3 (the normalized list is `1,4/3,1,1`), but is Poisson-normalized ULC: `k b_k^2 >= (k+1)b_(k-1)b_(k+1)` at `k=1,2` (`16>=6`, `18>=12`). One must specify which closure theorem, if any, is used. There is a simple exact perturbation bound: `B=L^3+z` implies `B^m >= binom(m,2) z^2 L^(3m-6)` coefficientwise for `m>=2`. Hence for `m>=5` and `m<=r<=2m+1`, with `b_(m,r)=[z^r]B^m`,

  `binom(3m+1,r-1)/b_(m,r) <= 2(3m+1)^7/[m(m-1)^2(m-4)^6] = O(m^-2)`.

  To verify: put `N=3m-6,k=r-2`; the ratio of the two binomial coefficients is `prod_(t=1)^7(N+t) / ((k+1)prod_(t=1)^6(N-k+t))`, and use `k+1>=m-1`, `N-k+t>=m-4`, and `binom(m,2)=m(m-1)/2`. The allowed claim snapshot records the verified r27 forest descent bound `Delta_x<0 => |T|<=4x`; since `|T_m|=4m+3`, it gives `x>=m+1`. Combined with eligibility, `m+1<=x<=p-2<=2m-1`, so the crossing rank really lies in this perturbation band. The estimate bounds the *size* of the perturbation there, not its coefficient differences or the LC margin of the polynomial sum. A route must prove curvature with an explicit margin, cover every later rank needed for the two deletion polynomials and `R`, and supply an effective cutoff. The retrieved De Angelis lead has not been applied with verified constants to these sums; no theorem is imported here.

## Six non-overlapping next-cycle avenues, in priority order

1. **T seat — both `T_m` orbit selectors.** Prove for every `m` with an eligible rank that `Delta_p D_0<0` and `Delta_p D_*<0`. Precise sufficient deliverable: LC tails of `D_0,D_*` from `x` and their two crossing-rank minors against `P`, with a proof of positivity/rank endpoints. A counterexample must give exact `m,p`, both coefficients, `x`, and the full selector. This isolates selector validity from the coupled aggregate.
2. **T seat — coupled `T_m` marked sign.** Independently prove `R_(p-1)<R_(p-2)` for every eligible `(m,p)`, without assuming orbit selection. A sufficient certificate is LC of `R` from `x` plus its crossing-rank minor against `P`; a stronger recurrence or quantitative coefficient certificate is acceptable if it gives explicit all-`m` inequalities and finite base cases. This isolates the genuinely coupled sign from the two selector tests and from r19's R-K shift.
3. **U seat — selected matching-slack charging.** Starting from (★), derive a non-tautological injection, discharging argument, or inequality for `Σ_Bw_F(B)(L_B-ν_B)+C` that reaches `(a-2p+1)Q` on a specified new order/rank class beyond `|T|<=2p+2`, or exhibit an exact selected cut obstructing the proposed inequality. The deliverable must name the class, preserve multiplicities and `F`, and show how positive T22 arm tags are compensated.
4. **F seat — heterogeneous four-arity transfer.** Use r19 F2's exact `H(c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4)` formulas to establish a *new intermediate* cross-arity comparison that couples `1[a_k(p)<0]` to the signed `g_k(p)` with `k n_k` weights. Deliver either a proved family theorem with a proof of that intermediate comparison, or an exact parameter/rank tuple refuting it with the complete selected aggregate. Restating F2's D6 as an assumption does not count.
5. **F seat — proper-selector structural witness.** Find an eligible ordinary tree with at least one selected and one unselected original leaf, or prove a nontrivial excluded subclass using the exact `Delta_p(T-v)` identities. Deliver the adjacency list, `a,x,p`, complete deletion-difference table, every `g_v`, and full `S`; distinguish a selector obstruction from a positive target counterexample. This decides whether all-leaf substitutions remain viable on a broad class.
6. **U seat — cross-tag unsigned operator obstruction/proof.** Define an explicit unsigned linear map on selected-tagged independent sets, with a proved equality connecting its rank defect to `S`, and test the exact T22 row plus one proper-selector row from avenue 5 if found. Deliver a genuine rank theorem beyond the first-shell band or an exact small kernel/cut obstruction to this **specific** operator. Do not reuse the signed boundary or claim generic edge weights solve fixed-selector capacity.

The neighborhood-component route is a reserve to avenue 3 if it yields a stronger weighted lower bound; otherwise it is another expression for addability. A pure saddle-point route is a reserve method for avenue 2 once it supplies explicit margins and an effective cutoff. These are allocation proposals for later T/F/U work, not new open registry keys or an authority award.
