## Independent comparison of three proofs

### Original marked-incidence proof — recommended

Work first on an arbitrary finite ambient set `U` of size `m` inside a simple graph, with marks `W` containing every vertex isolated **relative to U**. Let `q_j` be the number of independent `j`-subsets of `U` meeting `W`. For a marked independent `k`-set `A`, let `b(A)` count its neighbors *inside U*, put `B=Σ_A b(A)`, and let `C` count independent marked `(k+1)`-sets with at least two marks once each. Incidence counting gives, in `ℤ`,

`k q_(k+1)+C=(m-k)q_k-B`, hence `k(q_(k+1)-q_k)=(m-2k)q_k-B-C`.

When `m≤2k`, the right side is nonpositive. At `m=2k+1`, every lower set with `b(A)>0` contributes at least one to `B`. Every lower set with `b(A)=0` consists of relative isolates. If a non-isolated `y∈U` exists, `A↦A∪{y}` injects these bad sets into the family counted by `C`: `y∉A`, it is addable, and the image has at least `k≥2` marks because the isolate condition marks all of `A`. Thus `B+C≥q_k`. If `U` is edgeless, isolate coverage gives `U⊆W`, so `q_j=binom(m,j)` and the two middle layers at `m=2k+1` agree. Empty families and ranks above `m` are covered by the finset count, with integer arithmetic avoiding truncated subtraction. This establishes the generic scope without a bipartite or alpha cap. The source audit `preparation/C2-PREP-ORDER-AUDIT/REPORT.md` separately passed this same identity; my review checked its multiplicities and odd-boundary injection.

Use the relative-`U` lemma internally rather than transport an induced graph across a subtype. Its whole-graph instance `U=Finset.univ` is the first registered claim, with no extra terminal premise. For the tree, set `U=univ\C5LA1.H T v` and `W=C5LA1.R T v\C5LA1.H T v`. The original leaf is adjacent only to `s=C5LA1.support T v`; therefore `|U|=|T|-2≤2(p-1)+1`. Any vertex isolated in `T` on `U` must be adjacent to `s`: connectivity supplies a first edge on a path to `s`; a neighbor other than `s` would remain in `U`, while the deleted `v` cannot neighbor this vertex because its only neighbor is `s`. This proves relative isolate coverage. Since `H⊆R`, the pinned deletion counts split as `i_j(T-H)=q_j+i_j(T-R)` at every natural `j`. With `k=p-1`, `p≥3` gives `k≥2` and `k+1=p`; subtracting at `p` and `p-1` gives exactly the second registered inequality in `ℤ`. No leaf aggregation is needed for this terminal theorem.

### Ordered component root proof — valid direct ordinary-tree alternative

The `control/C2-ROOTED-MARK-ALTERNATIVE.md` proof is mathematically sound at its stated scope. Ordering marks, each marked independent set has a unique first mark `w_i`, and the remainder lies in `J_i=G-(N[w_i]∪{w_1,…,w_(i-1)})`. Thus `q_j=Σ_i i_(j-1)(J_i)`. The unmarked incidence bound gives `i_k(J_i)≤i_(k-1)(J_i)` when `|J_i|≤2k-1`, including out-of-range ranks after a separate zero case. If the first mark is non-isolated, each `J_i` loses at least two vertices from `G` and has that bound for `|G|≤2k+1`. In a tree leaf deletion, each component has exactly one root adjacent to `s`. A nontrivial component gives a non-isolated root; if every component is singleton, all vertices are marked and the binomial bound applies. This gives the registered ordinary claim. It does **not** alone prove the arbitrary-graph registered generic claim when all marks are isolates. Its component-root bookkeeping and a separate generic proof make it a longer joint intake despite being a good fallback for the leaf conjunct. The independent strategy audit in `preparation/C2-PREP-STRATEGY/REPORT.md` agrees, and explicitly separates the broader observed `p=2` argument from the registered `p≥3` claim.

### First-wide simplification — checked, but heavier to reuse

I independently checked the logic in `control-proposals/formal-intake/FIRST-SHELL-TWO-PART-ROUTE.md` A/B and `GENERIC-ORDER-BAND-VIA-FIRST-WIDE.md`. A marked shadow incidence proves any marks are nonincreasing at `m≤2k`. For `m≤2k+1`, `k≥2`, at least two edges, split at a chosen mark `w`. The residual marked family on `G-w` is under A. If `w` is non-isolated, `G-N[w]` has at most `2k-1` vertices and its ordinary layers descend. If `w` is isolated and `m=2k+1`, `J=G-w` has exactly `2k` vertices and still at least two edges; set `r=k-1≥1`. The **exact** old lemma `Erdos993G1.firstWide_nonpos_of_two_edges` has hypotheses `Nat.card J=2r+2` and at least two edges, so it supplies `i_k(J)≤i_(k-1)(J)` after the coefficient bridge. For at most one edge and isolate-covered `W`, an independent set avoiding `W` has size at most one: the unmarked vertices lie among the two adjacent endpoints of the only edge, or there are none. Since `k≥2`, the marked layers equal the ordinary layers and ordinary shadow counting closes them. Thus the simplification is valid, including empty `W` and out-of-range ranks. It is not the recommended *formal* route because the pinned G1 source has 25 declaration names shared with the first-interior source and would require an extracted dependency closure plus count/coefficient bridges.
