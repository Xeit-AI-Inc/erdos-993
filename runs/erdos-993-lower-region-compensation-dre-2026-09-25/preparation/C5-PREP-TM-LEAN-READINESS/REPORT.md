## Existing definitions and semantic bridge

The pinned `inputs/C4-first-shell-frozen-intake/BASE.lean` has reusable *actual* declarations. `C4LA1.IsGraphLeaf` (line 15) means exactly one adjacent vertex; `C5LA1.support` (line 29) chooses that original support on leaves. `C5LA1.H` (47) is `{v,s_v}` and `C5LA1.R` (60) is `insert s_v (G.neighborFinset s_v)`. `C5LA1.indepSetsAvoiding` (73), `indepSetCount` (86), and `forwardDifferenceDel` (99) count independent sets of the original graph avoiding a finite deletion set. `C4LA1.vertexDeletionIndepSetCount` (166), `vertexDeletionForwardDifference` (179), and `IsFavorableAt` (191) give the strict deletion selector. `C5LA1.leafSet` (205), `aggregate` (234), and `crossingIndex` (250) implement the fixed original-leaf sum and first strict descent. In particular, `aggregate` uses `p - 1` in `Nat`; the final contract must carry `x+2≤p`, hence `p≥2`, and prove this agrees with the intended integer rank. `Erdos993G1.indepCount`, `coeff`, `delta`, and `firstDescent` are another actual count/index encoding; their equivalence to the C5 natural-index versions is a lemma obligation, not definitional equality.

Mathlib itself supplies `SimpleGraph.IsNIndepSet`, `SimpleGraph.indepNum`, `SimpleGraph.indepSetFinset`, `SimpleGraph.isTree_iff_connected_and_card`, and `SimpleGraph.neighborFinset_eq_filter` in the inspected pinned files. `BASE.lean` is a frozen source snapshot, not a demonstrated importable module or a proof of the `T_m` graph formulas. A future formal run must register the exact reusable definitions or import an authorized verified module and bridge the chosen concrete graph to them. The R27 pinned theorem contract names the terminal `Erdos993G1.forest_descent_restriction` and an internal `forest_descent_linear_bound` lemma, but the contract alone is not a Lean import. The authoritative compiled declaration and its exact available name must be bound in the future run before applying `4m+3≤4x`.

Use a finite vertex type with three path vertices and `m` arm centers each with three distinct leaves (for example a tagged sum with `Fin m × Fin 4`), explicit symmetric irreflexive adjacency, and prove equivalence to the ordinary `T_m` description. Prove order `4m+3`, connectedness/acyclicity (`isTree_iff_connected_and_card` is an available route), `indepNum=3m+2`, and that the original leaf set consists of `a` plus all `3m` private leaves, with the specified supports. The `m≥1` domain matters: it excludes a degenerate path-only case and makes the two leaf orbits well-defined. Prove the four polynomial coefficient identities for `P=I(T_m)`, `A0=I(T_m-a)`, `Astar=I(T_m-c)`, and `Q=zR` by finite-set splits; this is the essential fidelity bridge from abstract polynomial arithmetic to `C5LA1.aggregate`. Preserve separate tags for private leaves sharing a support: `Q` has the multiplier `3m` because the sum is over leaves, not support fibers.

## Available algebra and missing ULC step

Actual Mathlib declarations inspected: `Polynomial.coeff_mul` is an antidiagonal convolution sum; `Polynomial.coeff_one_add_X_pow` identifies `((1+X)^n).coeff k` with `n.choose k`; `Polynomial.coeff_derivative` gives the adjacent coefficient times `n+1`; `Nat.choose_succ_succ`, `Nat.choose_pos`, and `Nat.choose_succ_right_eq` support binomial recurrence/ratio proofs. `Mathlib/Algebra/BigOperators/NatAntidiagonal.lean` contains antidiagonal sum recurrences. These are foundations, not a ready theorem that convolution preserves finite-order ULC. No ULC/Liggett or Cauchy–Binet declaration was found in the bounded searches. The mixed-minor formula from the candidate need not be expressed as a matrix theorem: prove the displayed double-sum antisymmetrization directly from `coeff_mul`, then show each bracket is nonnegative from log-concavity and consecutive support. That argument also needs careful zero-extension and boundary indices.

The proposed minimal large-`m` analytic split is: (1) define finite-order ULC over `ℚ` or `ℝ` with nonnegative coefficients and interval support, prove its convolution closure with every finite-order hypothesis used by the cited Liggett/Gurvits theorem, and verify the five factors at orders `1,1,4,2,2`; (2) derive the required ordinary LC margin and coefficient neighbor bounds for the factored main sequences; (3) prove the `h=8` coefficient lower bound and exact 27-factor quotient estimate with valid ranges; (4) prove the three mixed minors using the finite double-sum identity and separate perturbation bounds; (5) check the four strict rational cutoff comparisons by exact arithmetic. The choice `B` at ULC order **4**, despite degree 3, is mandatory; ULC order 3 would be false. An alternative bespoke LC closure theorem is possible, but it must replace the full strength actually used, including the quantitative margin. A declaration that merely assumes ULC closure is conditional and cannot earn the all-`m` award.

The numeric large-`m` obligation is exact, not a floating-point comparison: for `m≥1000`, `K=8!(125/124)^8(150/49)^27`, prove `ε=K/m^8` and `(2m+3)ε` is bounded at `m=1000`, then discharge the four constants `24`, `47`, `431`, and `14688/21` against 1. Natural subtraction in falling-factorial/rank bounds must be guarded before using `omega`, `ring`, or `norm_num`. The three main factorizations and their shifts differ, so a generic lemma must expose each degree, coefficient lower bound, and perturbation scale.

## Finite base and final propagation

The pinned Python replay checked `m=3..999`, `j=m..2m+2`, and the three auxiliary sequences: **1,507,464 positive LC margins and 1,507,464 positive mixed margins**, with zero failures. It also checked 276,938 eligible rows at `m=1..999`, but that redundant row check need not be formalized if propagation from the central margins is proved. For `m=1,2`, exact coefficients give `x=2,4`; the lower-region eligibility set is empty. A Lean finite-base certificate should define coefficient tables or a recurrence whose equality to the polynomial coefficients is proved, then kernel-check the bounded margin predicate. The Python recurrence from `B(B^m)'=mB'B^m` is a viable specification, but its divisions must be proved exact or avoided in a natural-number recurrence. A direct `decide` over millions of large-integer inequalities has not been benchmarked and may exhaust elaboration/kernel resources; split into bounded chunks or provide compact verified certificates. Do **not** use Python digest agreement as a proof, or `native_decide` without a separately approved trust/axiom policy. The inspected Mathlib linter explicitly says `native_decide` trusts the compiler; the pinned R27 contract forbids it. The finite base is mathematically decidable but kernel *feasibility is unverified* until a small representative chunk and the full bounded replay pass under the intended verifier.

Finally, prove `x≥m+1` from the verified R27 order bound by applying its terminal theorem with `r=x+1`, giving `4m+3≤4(x+1)-4=4x`; prove `x≤p-2≤2m-1` from lower-region eligibility (`p≤2m+1`). At `j=x`, a strict mixed minor and `Δ_x(P)<0` imply strict descent of each `A0,Astar,R` at rank `x`. Strict LC at subsequent central ranks propagates decreasing ratios through `p` (including endpoint `2m+2`); positivity of denominators must be explicit. Thus every original leaf is favorable, and the complete selected sum is `R_(p-1)-R_(p-2)<0`. This route proves the exact `C5LA1.aggregate≤0` statement for every eligible `p` and every `m≥1`; it does not rely on a pointwise assertion for arbitrary trees.

## Minimal honest theorem split and effort

The minimal top-level split is (A) graph/count bridge, (B) R27/index and region arithmetic bridge, (C) generic ULC/LC/mixed-minor algebra, (D) large-`m` estimates, (E) kernel-checked finite base, and (F) propagation plus the exact family theorem. These are **six independent proof-obligation packages**, with roughly 25–40 nontrivial bridge/estimate lemmas plus the ULC closure proof and the bounded computation certificate; this is an obligation count, not a timing prediction. The ULC closure and the full finite-base replay are the two feasibility gates. No conditional theorem assuming either package, no polynomial-only sign theorem, and no selected-orbit shortcut would constitute the ordinary all-`m` `T_m` aggregate award.

The pinned workflow requires a separately audited theorem contract, registered source, exact shared Mathlib binding, kernel build/single-file/axiom checks, and independent semantic fidelity receipt before closeout. This readiness report performs none of those stages and changes no registry state.
