## Exact target and why the focus moves

The primary registered OPEN target concerns a finite ordinary tree `T`, `a=alpha(T)`, the first strict zero-extended descent `x(T)`, and natural `p` satisfying `x(T)+2<=p` and the **strict** `3p<2a+1`. The fixed selected set consists of original leaves `v` with `Delta_p(T-v)<0`; its complete sum is

`S(T,p)=sum_(v selected) [Delta_(p-1)(T-{v,s_v})-Delta_(p-1)(T-N_T[s_v])]`.

The support, neighborhood and leaf tags are those of the original tree, including every leaf at a shared support. The sum is zero if no leaf is selected. It has no residual or governed-tree hypothesis. For `H_v=T-{v,s_v}`, `W_v=N_T(s_v)\{v}`, `R_v=H_v-W_v`, `k=p-1`, and `q_v(j)=i_j(H_v)-i_j(R_v)`, the summand is exactly `q_v(k+1)-q_v(k)`. The supplied incidence identity equivalently writes `kS=(2a+1-3p)sum_F q_v(k)-sum_F(D_v+C_v)`. The coefficient is positive in this region; `D_v,C_v>=0` alone gives no sign. Cycle 1's T and U adjudications give direct proofs of the identity and the leaf-support polynomial formulas, so another rediscovery has low value. Both admit the **global** missing budget, across selected leaf tags, as the actual obstruction.

Cycle 1's F adjudication retained finite negative full-sum searches, not an eligible positive witness. The order-91 `T_22` and order-243 `T_60` controls are eligible and have positive selected local terms but negative complete sums. Thus a pointwise or support-fibre sign, and a universal within-tag Hall matching, are stronger than the target and contradicted by admitted controls. The r28 order-22 `T22` slot-dominance counterexample is a different object and independently blocks a universal slot Hall premise. The registry keeps `E993-BETA-TARGET` and `E993-BETA-AGG-SUPPORT` REFUTED at their stated scopes; neither determines the lower-region complete ordinary sum. The all-rank ordinary R23 target is also a distinct OPEN key.

The registered ordinary-leaf order-band claim, if independently admitted, covers `|T|<=2p+1` pointwise. Eligibility implies `p>=3` for a nonempty ordinary tree because `Delta_0(T)=|T|-1>=0`, so its first uncovered order is `|T|=2p+2`. That shell has its own OPEN registered key. The finite p=6 instrument enumerated all 3,159 free trees at order 14 (`2p+2`), found 240 eligible rows, and reports all 240 complete sums negative, maximum `-222`; each eligible row was subset-replayed according to its sealed result. ## Independent audit of the ordered-mark alternative

Let `G` be any finite graph, let the marked set be ordered as distinct vertices `W={w_1,...,w_t}`, and let `q_j` count independent `j`-sets meeting `W`. Every such set has a unique first marked vertex `w_i`. After choosing it, the other `j-1` vertices must avoid `N_G[w_i]` and all earlier marks. Hence, with

`J_i=G-(N_G[w_i] union {w_1,...,w_(i-1)})`,

we have the exact partition `q_j=sum_i i_(j-1)(J_i)`. Later marked vertices remain available where nonadjacent; removing them would spoil the partition. The formula also holds when `W` is empty, giving zero on both sides.

For a graph `J` of order `N`, count `(B,u)` where `B` is independent of size `k-1` and `u` extends it. Each independent `k`-set has exactly `k` deletions, and each lower set has at most `N-k+1` extensions. Thus `k i_k(J)<=(N-k+1)i_(k-1)(J)` whenever `N>=k-1`. If `N<k-1`, both coefficients are zero, so `i_k<=i_(k-1)` still holds. In particular `N<=2k-1` and `k>=1` imply `i_k(J)<=i_(k-1)(J)`. This explicit split avoids treating a negative extension bound as a standalone cardinality bound.

If `m=|G|<=2k+1` and the first mark `y=w_1` is nonisolated, `|J_1|<=m-2` because `N[y]` contains two distinct vertices. For each `i>=2`, the deleted union contains both `y` and `w_i`, so `|J_i|<=m-2`. Consequently every term in `q_(k+1)=sum_i i_k(J_i)` is at most the corresponding term in `q_k=sum_i i_(k-1)(J_i)`. This is a valid *different* generic sufficient hypothesis: `W` contains a nonisolated vertex. It does not by itself prove the separately registered marked-isolate theorem when all marks are isolates and a nonisolated unmarked component exists.

For the leaf specialization, take an original leaf `v` with support `s` and `H=T-{v,s}`. Deleting `s` separates the tree into one component per original neighbor of `s`; deleting the singleton component `{v}` leaves `H`. Each remaining component contains exactly one vertex adjacent to `s`, its root in `W=N_T(s)\{v}`. It contains no second such vertex, since two would give a cycle through `s`. This proves the component-root fact, including the cases of zero remaining components and singleton components. If any component has an edge, its marked root has an incident edge in `H`; choose it first and use the preceding argument. If every component is a singleton, `H` is edgeless and `W=V(H)`. Then `q_j=binom(m,j)` for positive `j`, and for `m<=2k+1`, `binom(m,k+1)<=binom(m,k)`; the zero and out-of-range cases follow from zero extension. The `H`-empty case has both counts zero.

The rank shift is `q_(k+1)=q_p` and `q_k=q_(p-1)` for `k=p-1`. Since `|H|=|T|-2<=2p-1=2k+1`, the proof yields `q_p-q_(p-1)<=0`. This is exactly the registered ordinary-leaf summand for `p>=3`; in fact the argument works at `p=2`, but that is a broader unregistered observation and should not be silently substituted for the registered guard. No selector, first descent, alpha or lower-region assumption was used in this local proof. The `T=K_2` case has empty `H`; a one-vertex tree has no degree-one original leaf under the usual definition.

The alternative can plausibly shorten an ordinary-leaf Lean proof: it needs a first-mark partition, the elementary unmarked adjacent-rank shadow count, and the component-root lemma. The generic marked-isolate proof instead requires its extension-incidence identity, good/bad partition, injection of all-isolate lower sets into multiply marked upper sets, and edgeless complement case. This is a dependency comparison, not a measured Lean development or formal award. Formalization could still be dominated by finite-set bookkeeping and graph-deletion API details; no Lean was built under this brief. The generic marked-isolate proof remains useful for its wider graph scope and deserves its own independent audit (U2).

## What changes at the first shell

At `|T|=2p+2`, `H_v` has `m=2p=2k+2` vertices. An ordered first nonisolated root now gives only `|J_i|<=2k`, one vertex outside the automatic unmarked descent band. For a graph of order exactly `2k`, the same extension count gives only `i_k-i_(k-1)<=i_(k-1)/k`; it does **not** force nonpositivity. Thus the old order-band proof has a precise one-vertex gap rather than a generic unexplained failure.

The rooted-component fact sharpens that gap. Order a nontrivial-component root first. Any later root of a nontrivial component is in another component, so deleting its closed neighborhood removes its vertex and an internal neighbor, plus the earlier root: at least three distinct vertices. Also, every mark at position three or later removes at least three distinct marks. Consequently possible `|J_i|=2k` terms are confined to (i) the first root when its degree in `H` is one, and (ii) a singleton-component root placed second after that first root. If every component is a singleton, `H` is edgeless and the local binomial layer may rise at this shell; the tree's strict `x+2<=p` and favorable selector must be checked before treating it as an obstruction. These boundary observations are proposed proof inputs, not a sign result. They suggest a small, explicit defect to compensate across selected supports, which is why T1 and U3 take priority. In particular the controller's parameterized marked-isolate sharpness example at `m=2k+2` has a positive local term but `x=p` in its parent tree, so it is ineligible for the primary.
