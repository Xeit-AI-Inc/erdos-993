# Unawarded non-star branch lead

A concrete broader rooted branch class for T6 is the equal-length-three spider: root r joined to m disjoint paths r-a_i-b_i-c_i, m>=2. It has n=3m+1, alpha=2m and exactly m original leaves c_i. Put Q=1+3z+z^2 (the independence polynomial of a three-vertex path), V=1+2z. Root conditioning gives P=Q^m+zV^m. Deleting any endpoint gives A=V Q^(m-1)+z(1+z)V^(m-1). For its support b_i, direct support/closed-neighborhood subtraction gives q_i=zQ^(m-1), so if all leaves are selected then S=m Delta_(p-2)(Q^(m-1)). These formulas need independent graph checks; at m1 the root is also a leaf, so keep m>=2.

A sufficient new bridge is x(P)>=m-1. Eligibility would then give p>=m+1; the low-degree perturbation in A has degree m+1, while Q^(m-1) is symmetric and strictly LC with center m-1. These facts should imply every original leaf favorable and S<0 on eligible ranks. Prove the crossing bound or find its failure; do not assume a mode equals first descent. Ordinary forest bound n<=4x only gives about3m/4 and is insufficient here. No theorem or finite census is asserted by this lead; it is a concrete coefficient problem beyond pure star branches.

A possible different viewpoint is a symmetric-chain decomposition of the root-excluded product poset. The independent-set poset of one path a-b-c has the chain empty<a<{a,c} plus singleton chains {b},{c}, all centered at rank1; products have centered chains. A root-included j-set uses exactly j-1 branches (one of b,c in each), so for j<=m-2 it has at least3 empty branches. Removing root and replacing one designated empty branch by {a,c} gives an injective map into root-excluded (j+1)-sets when the designated branch is recoverable as the unique two-vertex branch. But this alone does not prove full rank monotonicity: its images must be kept disjoint from the root-excluded j-to-(j+1) injection. That precise disjointness/chain-capacity problem is the missing step, not an existing proof.
