# Quantitative large-powers lead for the next cycle

Root research lead, unawarded; do not insert into frozen Cycle4 reviews.

Primary source: Valerio De Angelis, Asymptotic expansions and positivity of coefficients for large powers of analytic functions, author PDF https://vdeangel.xula.edu/Publications/asy.pdf (retrieved 2026-09-26). Its Sections 3-5 discuss coefficient expansions under strong positivity, including a saddle radius in a compact positive interval; Section 6 addresses eventual log concavity. The introduction identifies explicit remainder bounds as part of the approach. This is a possible source for quantitative adjacent-ratio estimates, not an imported theorem about our sum of polynomials.

For B=1+4z+3z^2+z^3, strict triangle inequality holds on every positive-radius circle away from the positive axis, since positive coefficients include exponents0 and1. Its ordinary log concavity already follows directly, so eventual log concavity of B powers alone adds nothing. The useful missing result would be a uniform numerical curvature margin for the main coefficient sequence, large enough to absorb the binomial perturbation pointwise and retain the two original orbit selectors. Constants, endpoints, mode location, and a finite effective cutoff must be proved. The full tree polynomial and aggregate polynomial are sums with an m-dependent perturbation and are not single powers to which the source directly applies.

A second primary lead, Flajolet-Sedgewick Analytic Combinatorics, https://algo.inria.fr/flajolet/Publications/book.pdf, was found for large-powers saddle-point estimates (VIII.8), but the direct web open failed. Do not claim the theorem has been checked from that retrieval. No new status, no universal consequence from bounded coefficient tests.
