## C6 tail proposal: complete informal proof at exact scope

I find the proposed **strict** surplus proof valid for `m>=100` and every guarded rank, subject to the stated old full-Jensen and ratio-floor theorems. This is a new review conclusion, not a C5 result, all-m theorem, or Lean award. Let `c_k=choose(N,k)`, `E[k]=c_(k-1)`, `g=(k+1)(h-k+1)>0`, `lambda=(h+1)/g`, and `S_i=g C[k](lambda U_i[k]+M_k(E)/C[k])`. In the guard, `1<=k<=floor((N+2)/2)`, `h>=N+1`, and every denominator below is positive.

1. **Low band `4k<=N+1`.** The old center-subset expansion is `Q=sum_S z^s L^(N-R)`, with `R=sum_(i in S)r_i<=4s`. For a term present at adjacent ranks, cross multiplication of its normalized coefficients by `c_(k-1)c_k>0` reduces monotonicity to `s(N+1)-kR>=0`. A term newly appearing at `k` only helps. A term positive at `k-1` and absent at `k` would force `N-R=k-1-s` and `s<=k-1`; then `N+4<=4k`, impossible in this band. The empty term is constant one. Thus `Q[k]/c_k>=Q[k-1]/c_(k-1)`; likewise one rank earlier when `k>=2`. Binomial log-concavity gives `c_(k-1)Q[k-1]-c_k Q[k-2]>=0`. At `k=1`, `Q[-1]=0` handles that term. Expanding `C=Q+2zQ` yields `M_k(E)=c_(k-1)C[k]-c_k C[k-1]>=0`. Since `U_i[k]C[k]>0`, `S_i>0`. This also handles vanishing support and the smallest case `k=1`.
2. **Complement `4k>N+1`, `N>=200`.** For an unmarked block size `r`, put `g_r=(2r/(2r+1))choose(N-r,k-1)/choose(N,k)`. All binomial terms are positive here.
