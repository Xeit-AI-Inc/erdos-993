## Exact established result and proof assessment

For every finite ordinary connected acyclic simple graph T, set p=alpha(T)-2 as an integer. If the first strict negative forward difference x(T) satisfies x(T)+2<=p, then

`sum_{v original leaf, Delta_p(T-v)<0} [Delta_(p-1)(T-{v,s_v}) - Delta_(p-1)(T-N_T[s_v])] <= 0`.

Counts have integer zero extension; s_v and its closed neighborhood are original. Every selected leaf is a distinct tag, including leaves sharing a support. No residuality assumption is present. This exact primary is the sole registry award justified by this contract.

The key argument is a valid double count. For bipartite H and arbitrary W, let q_j count independent j-sets meeting W. A tagged (k+1)-set has at least k tag-preserving deletions. An independent k-set A has at most 2(alpha(H)-k) addable vertices: its addable induced graph is bipartite and has independence number at most alpha(H)-k. Thus `k q_(k+1)<=2(alpha(H)-k) q_k` for 1<=k<=alpha(H). The bound handles disconnected graphs, isolates, empty tags, terminal layers and zero counts, and never divides by q_k. This is unrelated to the refuted universal lower bound on per-set addability.

For an original leaf, H=T-{v,s}, W=N_T(s) minus {v}, and R=H-W. Hence the target term is exactly q_p-q_(p-1). Extending H-independent sets by v gives alpha(H)<=alpha(T)-1; in fact equality follows by removing at most one of the adjacent pair v,s from a maximum set of T, although the proof needs only the inequality. With alpha(T)>=7 and k=p-1=alpha(T)-3, the incidence coefficient is at most 4<=k, proving each original-leaf term nonpositive. The unchanged strict favorable filter selects a subset of those terms; it cannot create a positive sum. The absence of selector use in the sign argument is legitimate extra strength of the proof, not loss of target fidelity.

The eligibility exclusion is essential and sound. Eligibility initially forces alpha>=4 and p>=2. The tree identities i_1=n and i_2=C(n-1,2) exclude alpha=4,5. For alpha=6, x<=2 and n>=7.
