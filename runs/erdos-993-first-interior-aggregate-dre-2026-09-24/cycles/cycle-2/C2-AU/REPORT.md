## Self-contained proof at the exact target

All graphs here are finite simple graphs. For any graph `G`, `i_j(G)` counts independent `j`-subsets, extended by zero at integer ranks below zero or above `alpha(G)`; `Delta_j(G)=i_(j+1)(G)-i_j(G)`. A tree `T` is connected and acyclic. Put `a=alpha(T)`, integer `p=a-2`, and `x=min{j>=0: Delta_j(T)<0}`. The terminal rank has `Delta_a=-i_a<0`, so `x` exists. Assume the target eligibility `x+2<=p`. Because `x>=0`, integer arithmetic gives `p>=2`, `a>=4`, and `x<=a-4` before any natural-rank subtraction.

**Eligibility lemma.** In fact `a>=7`. Let `n=|V(T)|`. Since `a>=4`, `T` has an edge; otherwise connectedness would make it the one-vertex tree. Hence `n>=a+1`. A tree has `n-1` edges, so

`i_1=n`, `i_2=binom(n,2)-(n-1)=binom(n-1,2)`,

`Delta_0=n-1>0`, and `Delta_1=binom(n-1,2)-n`, which is positive for `n>=6` (it equals 4 at `n=6` and increases). If `a=4`, eligibility forces `x=0`, a contradiction. If `a=5`, it forces `x<=1`, also a contradiction.

For `a=6`, eligibility forces `x<=2` and `n>=7`, so the first two differences are positive. Count the independent triples by inclusion-exclusion over the edge-containing triple events. Each of the `n-1` edges belongs to `n-2` triples. Two such edge events intersect exactly when the two tree edges share a vertex; these pairs are counted by `sum_u binom(deg(u),2)`. There is no triple containing three tree edges. Thus

`i_3=binom(n,3)-(n-1)(n-2)+sum_u binom(deg(u),2)`,

`Delta_2=(n-1)(n-2)(n-9)/6+sum_u binom(deg(u),2)`.

For `n>=9`, both terms in the last expression are nonnegative. For `n=7`, the complement of an independent six-set is a one-vertex cover of all six edges. Connectedness makes `T=K_(1,6)`, so `Delta_2=-10+binom(6,2)=5`. For `n=8`, the two-vertex complement `{u,w}` of an independent six-set covers all seven edges, hence `deg(u)+deg(w)>=7` (an edge `uw`, if present, is counted twice, which only increases this sum). For nonnegative integer degrees with sum at least seven, convexity or a direct integer check gives `binom(deg(u),2)+binom(deg(w),2)>=binom(3,2)+binom(4,2)=9`. The first term is `-7`, so `Delta_2>=2`. No possible `n` has a strict negative difference by index two. Thus `a=6` is impossible and `a>=7`. This proof needs neither r27 nor numerical experimentation. [Sources: `cycles/cycle-2/C2-U1/REPORT.md`, `C2-U3/REPORT.md`, and their four assigned U1/U3 critique reports.]

**Tagged incidence lemma.** Let `H` be any finite bipartite graph, `W⊆V(H)`, `h=alpha(H)`, and `q_j` the number of independent `j`-sets of `H` meeting `W`, with the same integer zero extension. For each integer `1<=k<=h`, count pairs `(A,B)` where `A⊂B`, `A` and `B` are independent and meet `W`, and `|A|=k`, `|B|=k+1`. A fixed `B` has at least `k` valid deletions: at most the deletion of its sole `W` vertex can destroy the tag, and when it has two `W` vertices every deletion works. Therefore the incidence count is at least `k q_(k+1)`. For a fixed `A`, every extension vertex lies in the induced graph `K=H-N_H[A]`, deleting the **closed** neighborhood of `A`. Every independent set of `K` joins `A`, so `alpha(K)<=h-k`. Each color class of the bipartite `K` is independent, whence `|V(K)|<=2alpha(K)<=2(h-k)`. This bounds even extensions that would fail a hypothetical upper tag restriction, so the incidence count is at most `2(h-k)q_k`. Consequently

`k q_(k+1) <= 2(h-k) q_k`.

No division by `q_k` occurs. If `q_k=0`, the deletion lower bound forces `q_(k+1)=0`. At `k=h`, the upper layer is empty. If `k>h`, both layers are zero and the variant `k q_(k+1)<=2(a-1-k)q_k` remains the true equality `0<=0` whenever `h<=a-1`, even if the displayed coefficient is negative. Empty `W`, empty `H`, and disconnected bipartite `H` cause no exception. This proves both incoming tagged lemmas at their exact statements; `C2-U1`'s parameter `a` is obtained from `h<=a-1`. [Sources: `cycles/cycle-2/C2-U1/REPORT.md`, `C2-CT-U2/REPORT.md`, and the T/F critiques of U1, U2 and U3.]

**Leaf binding and complete sum.** Fix *any original degree-one vertex* `v` of `T`, with its unique original support `s`. Set `H_v=T-{v,s}` and `W_v=N_T(s)\{v}`. The set `W_v` is a subset of `V(H_v)`: `s` is excluded by simplicity and `v` by definition. The original closed-neighborhood deletion is exactly `R_v=T-N_T[s]=H_v-W_v`. Independent sets of `H_v` partition into those meeting `W_v` and those avoiding it, and the latter are exactly the independent sets of `R_v`. Thus at every integer rank `j`, `q_j=i_j(H_v)-i_j(R_v)`, including rank zero and zero-extended ranks. Direct subtraction gives the *contract's* summand

`Delta_(p-1)(H_v)-Delta_(p-1)(R_v)=q_p-q_(p-1)`.

Every independent set of `H_v` extends by `v` to one of `T`, since `v` was adjacent only to the deleted `s`; hence `h=alpha(H_v)<=a-1`. Put `k=p-1=a-3`. The eligibility lemma gives `k>=4`. If `k>h`, both tagged counts vanish. Otherwise the incidence lemma and `h<=a-1` give

`k q_p <= 2(h-k)q_(p-1) <= 2((a-1)-(a-3))q_(p-1)=4q_(p-1)<=k q_(p-1)`.

Since `k>0`, this implies `q_p<=q_(p-1)`. Hence **every original-leaf summand is nonpositive**, whether or not that leaf is favorable. The favorable set is exactly `F_p(T)={v original leaf: Delta_p(T-v)<0}`, at the unshifted original rank and with strict inequality. Selecting any subset of nonpositive leaf terms and summing once for each original leaf gives `S(T,p)<=0`; two leaves at one support remain two terms. Empty favorable set gives zero. No residual hypothesis, Hall condition, sign of a deleted-tree selector, or forest aggregate theorem is used. This closes the informal mathematical dependency DAG: direct eligibility lemma + tagged incidence lemma + elementary original-leaf set identities + integer arithmetic ⇒ exact primary sign.

The high-tail corollary discussed in the sources is broader than the primary: for a bipartite graph with an original leaf, `p>=2`, and `3p>=2alpha(G)+1`, put `k=p-1` and use `h<=alpha(G)-1` to get `2(h-k)<=k`; if `k>h` both terms vanish. At `p=1`, the guard forces `alpha(G)<=1`, and the presence of a degree-one vertex forces `G=K2`; then `H` is empty and `q_0=q_1=0`. These endpoints are not used at the eligible target, where `p>=5`. At `p=a-2`, the high-tail guard is exactly `a>=7` because `3(a-2)>=2a+1` iff `a>=7`. No assertion is made at lower ranks.
