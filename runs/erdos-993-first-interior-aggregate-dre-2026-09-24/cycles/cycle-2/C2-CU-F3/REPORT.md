## C2-F3-002 — proposed retained

Eligibility is an integer statement: `p=a-2` and `x+2<=p` imply `x<=a-4`, hence `a>=4`. For a nontrivial tree of order `n`, `Delta_0=n-1>0`, `i_2=C(n-1,2)`, and `Delta_1=C(n-1,2)-n`. At `a=4`, `x<=0` is impossible. At `a=5`, `n>=6`, so `Delta_1>=4` and `x<=1` is impossible. At `a=6`, `n>=7`, `Delta_1>0`, and eligibility would require `Delta_2<0`.

The exact tree triple formula is `i_3=C(n,3)-(n-1)(n-2)+sum_u C(deg(u),2)`. It counts all triples, subtracts those containing an edge, and restores triples containing two adjacent edges; a triple in a tree cannot contain three edges. Subtracting `i_2` gives `Delta_2=(n-1)(n-2)(n-9)/6+sum_u C(deg(u),2)`. For `n>=9` this is nonnegative. At `n=7`, a six-set independent complement is one vertex covering all edges, hence the connected tree is `K_(1,6)` and `Delta_2=5`. At `n=8`, the complement is a two-vertex edge cover `{u,w}`. Every one of the seven edges is incident to `u` or `w`, so `deg(u)+deg(w)>=7` even if their connecting edge is counted twice. Convexity of `C(d,2)` gives `C(deg(u),2)+C(deg(w),2)>=C(3,2)+C(4,2)=9`. The base term is `-7`, hence `Delta_2>=2`. This exhausts `a<=6`. The proof uses ordinary tree connectedness in these cover cases; it does not establish a forest analogue.
