## New lemma C2-CT-U2-TAGGED-SHADOW-PROOF (proposed informal proof)

Let `H` be any finite bipartite simple graph, `W⊆V(H)`, `a=alpha(H)`, `1<=k<=a`, and `q_j` count independent `j`-sets meeting `W`. Then

`k q_(k+1) <= 2(a-k) q_k`.

**Proof.** Count incidences `(A,B)` with `A⊂B`, `|A|=k`, `|B|=k+1`, both independent and meeting `W`. Every tagged `B` has at least `k` admissible deletions: if it contains exactly one `W` vertex, delete any of the other `k` vertices; if at least two, all `k+1` deletions work. Hence the incidence count is at least `k q_(k+1)`. Fix a tagged independent `A` of size `k`. Its addable vertices form `E=V(H)\N_H[A]`. Choose a bipartition of the induced graph `H[E]` as `E_0⊔E_1`; each part is independent and has no edge to `A`. Thus both `A∪E_0` and `A∪E_1` are independent in `H`, so `|E_i|<=a-k`, and `|E|<=2(a-k)`. Every extension of `A` by one vertex in `E` remains tagged because `A` already meets `W`. Summing this upper bound over the `q_k` choices of `A` gives the displayed inequality. This includes disconnected graphs and empty `W`; at `k=a`, `E` is empty. No tree, leaf, residual, or favorable hypothesis is used. □

For a bipartite `G` with original leaf `v`, support `s`, write `H=G-{v,s}` and `W=N_G(s)\{v}` inside `H`; exactly `R=H-W=G-N_G[s]`. Thus `q_j=i_j(H)-i_j(R)` and the leaf summand at integer rank `p>=1` is `q_p-q_(p-1)`. Every independent set in `H` extends by `v`, so `a_H<=alpha(G)-1`. For `p>=2`, set `k=p-1`. If `k>a_H`, `q_k=q_(k+1)=0`; otherwise the lemma applies, and `3p>=2alpha(G)+1` gives `3k>=2a_H`, hence `2(a_H-k)<=k`; therefore `q_p<=q_(p-1)`. For the broader pointwise statement's endpoint `p=1`, its guard implies `alpha(G)<=1`. A graph with a degree-one vertex and independence number 1 must be `K2`: any additional vertex would either violate alpha 1 or, if joined to all vertices, violate the leaf's degree 1. Then `H` and `R` are empty and the term is zero. The eligible primary only uses `p>=2`.

## New lemma C2-CT-U2-ELIGIBILITY-PROOF (proposed informal proof)

For every ordinary finite connected tree satisfying target eligibility, `alpha(T)>=7`.

**Proof.** Write `a=alpha(T)`, `n=|V(T)|`, `x=x(T)`, and `p=a-2`. Eligibility gives `a>=4` and `x<=a-4`. The pinned r27 LA4 forest bound, after the coefficient/first-descent bijection above, applies at the *actual* strict descent `x` and gives `n<=4x<=4(a-4)`. Since `a<=n`, values `a=4,5` are impossible. If `a=6`, then `6<=n<=8`. A connected graph with an edge cannot have `a=n`, so `n=7` or `8`. In a tree, `i_1=n` and `i_2=binom(n,2)-(n-1)`, equal to 15 and 21 respectively for `n=7,8`. Thus `Delta_0=n-1>0` and `Delta_1=i_2-n>0`. Since eligibility gives `x<=2`, we must have `x=2` and `Delta_2<0`.

For `n=7`, an independent six-set has a one-vertex complement covering every edge. Connectedness forces the tree to be `K_(1,6)`. Its `i_2=15`, `i_3=20`, so `Delta_2=5>0`, contradiction. For `n=8`, choose an independent six-set `A`; its two-vertex complement `C` covers every edge. The 20 triples in `A` are independent, while `i_2=21` and `Delta_2<0` imply `i_3<=20`. Consequently no triple consisting of a vertex `c∈C` and two vertices of `A` is independent. Each `c` has at most one nonneighbor in `A`, hence at least five neighbors in `A`. The two vertices of `C` force at least ten distinct edges, exceeding the seven edges of an eight-vertex tree. Contradiction. Therefore `a>=7`. □
