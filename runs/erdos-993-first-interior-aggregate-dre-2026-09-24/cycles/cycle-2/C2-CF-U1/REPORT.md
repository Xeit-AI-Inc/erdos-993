## `C2-U1-TAGGED-SHADOW-COUNT`: hostile proof audit

Let `H` be finite bipartite, `W` arbitrary, and `q_j` count independent `j`-subsets of `H` meeting `W`. First, `R=H-W` really makes `q_j=i_j(H)-i_j(R)`: an independent set in `H` either avoids `W` and belongs to `R` or meets `W`. This remains true for disconnected `H`, empty `W`, rank zero, and zero-extended out-of-range ranks. For an original leaf `v` with support `s`, `H=T-{v,s}` and `W=N_T(s)\{v}` lie in `H`, while `R=T-N_T[s]=H-W`. Thus the requested summand is exactly `q_p-q_(p-1)`, with no selector or support-tag alteration.

For `k>=1`, count incidences `(A,B)` with `A` a tagged independent `k`-set, `B` a tagged independent `(k+1)`-set, and `A⊂B`. A tagged `B` loses its tag under at most one vertex deletion: only a unique member of `B∩W` could be removed. Hence it supplies at least `k` incidences. A tagged `A` extends only by vertices in `K=H-N_H[A]`, the graph after deleting the **closed** neighborhood of `A`. Each independent set of `K` augments `A`; hence `alpha(K)<=alpha(H)-k<=a-1-k`. Bipartiteness makes each of the two color classes independent, so `|V(K)|<=2alpha(K)<=2(a-1-k)`. This bounds all possible one-vertex extensions, including those not tagged. Double counting gives `k q_(k+1)<=2(a-1-k)q_k`.

I tested the vulnerable cases explicitly. A single witness in `B∩W` leaves exactly `k` valid deletions, not zero. If `q_k=0` but `q_(k+1)>0`, those deletions give a contradiction, so no division by a possibly zero count is hidden. If `k>alpha(H)`, both q-counts vanish; even when `2(a-1-k)` is negative, its product with `q_k=0` is zero. If `k=alpha(H)`, the upper layer is empty and the extension bound is zero. Empty `W`, empty `H`, and disconnected `H` are harmless. The integer parameter `a` cannot make the nonempty-layer bound negative because any nonempty `Q_k` forces `k<=alpha(H)<=a-1`.

For a leaf of a tree, `H` is a bipartite forest and every independent set of `H` extends by the deleted leaf, so `alpha(H)<=alpha(T)-1`. With `a=alpha(T)>=7`, `p=a-2`, and `k=p-1=a-3>=4`, the count gives `k q_p<=4q_(p-1)`. Since `q_p>=0` and `k>=4`, it follows that `q_p<=q_(p-1)`. This is pointwise for **every original leaf**, including leaves not selected by `Delta_p(T-v)<0`. It does not infer or replace the selector. At the broader auxiliary guard `3p>=2a+1`, the same arithmetic is `p-1>=2(a-p)` for `p>=2`. At `p=1`, the guard forces `a<=1`; a graph with a degree-one leaf then has `a=1` and empty `H`, making the term zero. This separate case supports the report's precise high-tail remark but is not needed for the incoming primary-rank claim.

## `C2-U1-ELIGIBILITY-ALPHA-LOWER-BOUND`: hostile proof audit

Eligibility is integer arithmetic: `x+2<=p=a-2` gives `x<=a-4`, and `x>=0` forces `a>=4` before any natural subtraction. For an `n`-vertex tree, `i_1=n` and `i_2=binom(n,2)-(n-1)=binom(n-1,2)`. Thus `Delta_0=n-1` and `Delta_1=binom(n-1,2)-n`. The source's triple formula is exact: the `n-1` edges yield `(n-1)(n-2)` edge/triple incidences; the only double-counted edge-containing triples are wedges, each with a unique center because the tree has no triangle. Consequently

`Delta_2=(n-1)(n-2)(n-9)/6 + sum_u binom(deg(u),2)`.

For `a=4`, eligibility would require `x<=0`, contrary to `Delta_0>0` (`n>=5`). For `a=5`, it would require `x<=1`, contrary to `Delta_0,Delta_1>0` (`n>=6`; `Delta_1=4` at `n=6` and grows). For `a=6`, it would require `x<=2`. Here `n>=7` and the first two differences are positive. At `n>=9`, `Delta_2>=0`. At `n=7`, the complement of an independent 6-set is a one-vertex edge cover, so the tree is `K_(1,6)` and `Delta_2=-10+15=5`. At `n=8`, the complement is a two-vertex cover of seven edges. The covering vertices have degree sum at least seven, and for nonnegative integer degrees with sum at least seven the minimum `binom(d_1,2)+binom(d_2,2)` is nine. Hence `Delta_2>=-7+9=2`. Thus `x<=2` is impossible. The case split correctly excludes all `a<7`; flat differences are treated as non-descents. Singleton and two-vertex trees cannot satisfy eligibility.

Combining the two retained claims gives an informal all-order obstruction to a primary counterexample: every eligible tree has `a>=7`, each original leaf term is nonpositive, and a sum over the **complete** strictly favorable original-leaf set is therefore nonpositive. Empty favorable sets give zero; repeated support leaves remain repeated tags. This uses neither residuality nor a shifted rank. It is a proof of impossibility at every finite order conditional only on the two audited informal arguments, not an extrapolation from finite data.
